## Dire Wolf — Aenocyon dirus

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : EE… noh… SY… on. In Greek, "cyon" means dog. [serious] And "dirus," in Latin, means terrible, fearsome. The terrible dog.
**WEX** [confident] : A big wolf?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : People call it the Dire Wolf, yes — [serious] but not a dinosaur: it lived way later, in the Ice Age, [pauses] fifty thousand years ago, in North and South America.
**WEX** [curious] : Who did it hunt alongside?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Often in packs, just its own kind. [warmly] And Smilodon, not too far off, hunted the same prey.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : It was 5.5 feet long — like a tall Dad lying on the ground! Standing up, it was about 3 feet tall — [amazed] as tall as a 4-year-old! And it weighed 70 kilos — [proud] as heavy as a kangaroo!
**WEX** [curious] : Bigger than a wolf today?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Stockier, mostly. [proud] A bigger head, stronger teeth than a gray wolf.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : It was a carnivore. It hunted wild horses in packs, [amazed] bison, and young animals — never alone.
**WEX** [curious] : Did it fight with Smilodon?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Sometimes, yes, over the same prey. [warmly] Two big hunters, sharing the same icy ground.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [amazed] : For a long time, everyone thought it was just a really big gray wolf. [pauses] But scientists studied its DNA, not long ago.
**WEX** [gasps] : And then?
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : It wasn't even a true wolf at all — [amazed] just a distant cousin, its own family, all alone.
