## Albertosaurus — Albertosaurus sarcophagus

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Al… bur… toh… saurus. [curious] "Alberto" comes from Alberta, a BIG region of Canada where its bones were found. [playful] And "saurus," you know what that means?
**WEX** [confident] : Lizard. So… the Alberta lizard.
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Exactly. [amazed] It lived there, seventy million years ago. [warmly] A cousin of T. rex.
**WEX** [curious] : Did it have enemies? [nervous] Hunters even tougher than it?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : No. [warmly] In its own country, it was the biggest hunter around. [proud] No other carnivore was big enough to mess with it.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : It was 9.5 meters long — as long as a truck! [quickly] Standing up, it was 3 meters tall — as tall as a basketball hoop! And it weighed 2,000 kilos — as heavy as a rhino! [amazed] And scientists think it could run about 19 miles an hour — as fast as a horse cantering.
**WEX** [curious] : What about its arms? [playful] Tiny, like T. rex?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Yes, just the same: tiny, [amazed] with only TWO fingers on each hand.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : It was a carnivore. It hunted the big plant-eaters of its country — [confident] Edmontosaurus, and Saurolophus with its crest. [pauses] And maybe it didn't hunt alone: [amazed] scientists found 26 Albertosaurus together, in one spot.
**WEX** [gasps] : 26? So they were friends?
**NARRATEUR H** [playful] : That's exactly what scientists are trying to figure out. [mischievously] Maybe a real pack, like wolves today. [curious] They're still searching for the answer.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Its full scientific name is Albertosaurus sarcophagus. [confident] You already know Albertosaurus. [whispers] And "sarcophagus," in Greek, means… flesh-eating.
**WEX** [playful] : Right there in its name.
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : Yes. [amazed] With teeth like that, the name fit perfectly.
