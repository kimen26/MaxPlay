## Brachiosaurus — Brachiosaurus altithorax

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Brak… ee… oh… SOR… us. [curious] In Greek, "brachio" means arm. And "saurus," [pauses] lizard.
**WEX** [curious] : The arm lizard?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Exactly. Its front legs [pauses] were longer than its back legs — like two giant arms. It lived in North America, [amazed] a hundred fifty million years ago.
**WEX** [playful] : Was it all alone out there?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : No, in herds. And hunters like Allosaurus [serious] only dared go after the young ones — a full-grown adult was way too tall for that.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Seventy-two feet long — as long as two school buses. Standing up, [pauses] forty-three feet tall — as tall as a 4-story building. And forty-seven thousand kilos — [amazed] as heavy as NINE elephants.
**WEX** [gasps] : Nine elephants?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Yep. But here's the thing, [pauses] it's not even the tail that makes it so long: it's the neck, stretched straight up toward the sky.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : Herbivore. It ate leaves way up at the top of the trees — [pauses] the ones nobody else could reach. [confident] No one fought it for that meal.
**WEX** [curious] : How did it digest leaves that tough?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : It swallowed stones. [pauses] They stayed in its stomach and ground up its food, like a mill.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Its head reached all the way up to forty-three feet — [pauses] as tall as a 4-story building, [amazed] just its head.
**WEX** [amazed] : So it could see over the trees?
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : It could see over everything. [warmly] A gentle giant, head in the clouds.
