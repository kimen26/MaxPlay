## Camarasaurus — Camarasaurus lentus

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Kam… AR… uh… SOR… us. [curious] In Greek, "kamara" means a chamber — a little room.
**WEX** [curious] : The chambered lizard?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Yep — its bones had hollow spaces inside, [amazed] like tiny little rooms. It lived in North America, a hundred fifty-five million years ago.
**WEX** [hesitant] : Was this one rare?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Just the opposite! [proud] It was the most common sauropod in that whole region, back then.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Fifty feet long — [amazed] as long as a school bus. Standing up, twenty feet tall — [curious] as tall as a street lamp. And eighteen thousand kilos — [proud] as heavy as 6 hippos.
**WEX** [gasps] : 6 hippos.
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Yep. [whispers] A big head, and thick teeth for chewing through the toughest branches.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : Herbivore, in herds, with its babies. [calm] Its skull had big window-like openings, to keep it light.
**WEX** [nervous] : So who attacked it?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Allosaurus — but mostly the ones that wandered off from the group. [serious] Right in the middle of the herd, an adult was pretty safe.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Scientists found a baby Camarasaurus almost ENTIRELY whole — [amazed] one of the most complete baby sauropod skeletons ever discovered.
**WEX** [amazed] : So tiny, and already whole.
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : Thanks to it, [proud] scientists know exactly what a baby of this family looked like.
