## Deinonychus — Deinonychus antirrhopus

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Dye… NON… ih… kus. In Greek, "deino" means terrible — [serious] same root as the word "dinosaur"! And "onyx" is claw. [curious] So what does that give us?
**WEX** [confident] : The terrible claw.
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : There you go. [proud] It lived in North America, one hundred fifteen million years ago.
**WEX** [curious] : Is this the big scary raptor [gasps] from the movies?
**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : Yes, actually [gently] it's this one that inspired those images — not the small Velociraptor. But this one had feathers too.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : It was 3.4 meters long — [quickly] like a small car! Standing up, it was 0.9 meters tall — as tall as a 4-year-old! And it weighed 80 kilos — [proud] as heavy as Dad. [amazed] And scientists think it could run about 19 miles an hour — as fast as a horse cantering.
**WEX** [curious] : And its famous claw, [gasps] how big was it?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : About 5 inches. [serious] Curved, razor-sharp — a real weapon for hooking prey.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : It was a carnivore. It hunted dinosaurs much bigger than itself, [confident] like Iguanodon, and maybe not alone.
**WEX** [curious] : Not alone?
**NARRATEUR H** [hesitant] : Scientists still debate it. [slowly] Several Deinonychus have been found near the same big prey — [pauses] maybe they hunted in groups to take down something bigger than them.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Its curved claw was made for hooking, [amazed] like a grappling hook. [slowly] It would jump on its prey, plant its claw, and hold on with its long-feathered arms while its teeth did the rest.
**WEX** [gasps] : A living grappling hook.
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : Exactly. [proud] That claw is what gave its name to the whole raptor family.
