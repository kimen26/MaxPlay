## Dimetrodon — Dimetrodon grandis

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [curious] : Dimetrodon. "Di," in Greek, means two. [serious] "Metron," measure — like in "meter." And "odon," tooth.
**WEX** [confident] : Two-measure teeth?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Right — he had two different sizes of teeth in his mouth. He lived two hundred eighty million years ago, [amazed] in North America.
**WEX** [curious] : Is he a dinosaur, if he lived that long before them?
**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : No, actually — not at all. He lived fifty million years BEFORE the first dinosaurs. [warmly] He's a very ancient cousin [confident] of furry animals, like us.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : He was 3 and a half meters long — like a small car! [amazed] Standing up, he was as tall as Dad standing up! And he weighed 250 kilos — [serious] as heavy as a tiger!
**WEX** [gasps] : As tall as Dad?
**NARRATEUR H** [chuckles] : As tall, yes — [proud] mostly thanks to his sail, standing up tall on his back.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : He was a lone hunter, catching small reptiles. [confident] He had to watch out for the other big hunters of his time.
**WEX** [curious] : And his sail, what was it for when he hunted?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Not really for hunting — more for warming up. [calm] In the morning, he'd turn toward the sun, sail spread wide, like a living radiator.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [amazed] : Dimetrodon isn't even a reptile like the others. [serious] He's a very, very distant ancestor of furry animals — [warmly] a distant cousin of ours.
**WEX** [gasps] : A cousin of ours, with a sail on his back?
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : A very, very distant cousin, yes — [warmly] but a real family link, all the same.
