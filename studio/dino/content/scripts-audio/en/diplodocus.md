## Diplodocus — Diplodocus longus

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Dih… PLOD… oh… kus. In Greek, "diplo" means [curious] double. And "dokos," a beam — [pauses] a big wooden bar.
**WEX** [curious] : The double beam?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Yep — because of the [amazed] shape of the bones in its tail. It lived in North America, a hundred fifty million years ago.
**WEX** [hesitant] : How did it defend itself, with such a long neck?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : With its tail, actually. [serious] Allosaurus and Ceratosaurus preferred going after young ones who wandered off — [calm] a tight herd was a whole different story.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Eighty-five feet long — [amazed] like TWO buses, one behind the other. Standing up, fifteen feet tall — [curious] as tall as a double-decker bus. And twelve thousand kilos — [proud] as heavy as 4 hippos.
**WEX** [gasps] : Two whole buses.
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Yep. [whispers] And out of those eighty-five feet, the tail alone is almost half.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : Herbivore, in a big herd. [calm] It grazed on low plants with its long neck held almost flat.
**WEX** [curious] : And what else was its tail good for, besides defense?
**NARRATEUR H** [playful] : It cracked it like a whip, super fast. [whispers] Scientists think it could have made a sound as loud as thunder.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Out of its eighty-five feet, the tail alone measures [amazed] about forty-two feet — half its whole body.
**WEX** [amazed] : Half, just the tail.
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : A giant whip-tail, [proud] on the end of an equally long neck. An animal built out of extremes.
