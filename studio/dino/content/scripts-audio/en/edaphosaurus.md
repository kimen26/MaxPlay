## Edaphosaurus — Edaphosaurus pogonias

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [curious] : ed… ah… foh… SOR… us. "Edaphos," in Greek, means the ground, the paving stone — [serious] flat rock. And "saurus," lizard.
**WEX** [confident] : The paving-stone lizard?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Close — the lizard with paving-stone teeth. [amazed] He had dozens of little teeth packed tight, [serious] like paving stones in a street, for crushing plants.
**WEX** [curious] : Where did he live?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : In Texas, two hundred eighty million years ago — [serious] way before the first dinosaurs. He's not a dinosaur either.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : He was 3 meters long — like three 4-year-olds lying end to end! [amazed] He was as tall as a car — you'd have to look up! And he weighed 120 kilos — [serious] as heavy as a pig!
**WEX** [gasps] : Three kids lying down at once?
**NARRATEUR H** [chuckles] : Three kids lying down, yes — [confident] end to end, that's his whole length.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : He grazed on ferns, calm and steady, with his little paving-stone teeth. [confident] He lived in the same place as another animal, [curious] with an almost identical sail on its back.
**WEX** [curious] : Who was that?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Dimetrodon. His sail looks a lot like this one — [playful] but watch out, it's the total opposite underneath.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [amazed] : Dimetrodon ate meat. [serious] Edaphosaurus, though, ate no meat at all — only plants. [confident] Same sail, completely opposite diets.
**WEX** [gasps] : Two almost-identical sails, but one eats meat and the other eats plants?
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : Exactly. The shape of an animal [confident] doesn't always tell you what it eats.
