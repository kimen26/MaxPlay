## Edmontonia — Edmontonia rugosidens

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : ed… mon… TOH… nee… uh. This name doesn't come from Greek — [pauses] it comes from a place.
**WEX** [curious] : A place?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Yep, it was found near the city of Edmonton, in Canada. [amazed] So they called it "the one from Edmonton." [calm] It lived seventy-two million years ago, covered head to tail in armor.
**WEX** [amazed] : Like Ankylosaurus?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : A cousin, yes. [curious] But with one big difference, you'll see.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Twenty-two feet long — [amazed] as long as a road is wide — it takes up the whole street. Standing up, six and a half feet tall — [curious] as tall as a door. And three thousand kilos — [proud] as heavy as a hippo.
**WEX** [gasps] : A hippo.
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Yep. [whispers] A real fortress on legs, but flat and low to the ground.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : Herbivore. [calm] It grazed on low plants, calmly. It mostly lived alone.
**WEX** [nervous] : Who dared attack it?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Gorgosaurus, and a T. rex cousin called Daspletosaurus. [serious] But with those spikes on its shoulders, [pauses] attacking from the side could go badly for the hunter.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Edmontonia had big pointed spikes on its shoulders — [serious] but no club at the end of its tail like Ankylosaurus.
**WEX** [hesitant] : So how did it defend itself?
**NARRATEUR H** [playful] : It lowered its head and pointed those spikes forward. [confident] Impossible to grab without getting poked.
