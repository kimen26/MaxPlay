## Edmontosaurus — Edmontosaurus regalis

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : ed… MON… toh… SOR… us. [curious] His name comes from Edmonton, a city in Canada where his bones were found. And "saurus" is Greek for lizard.
**WEX** [confident] : The Edmonton lizard.
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Exactly. [warmly] He lived in North America, sixty-eight million years ago — at the same time as Triceratops.
**WEX** [curious] : Did he have friends?
**NARRATEUR H** [playful] : Thousands. [amazed] Enormous herds. And T. rex watched them, patiently.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : He was 12 meters long — as long as a school bus! 3 meters 50 tall — [quickly] like two Dads standing on each other's shoulders! And 4,000 kilos — [amazed] as heavy as 2 rhinos!
**WEX** [gasps] : As long as a bus?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Yes, twelve meters of meat and bone. [warmly] A gentle giant with a duck bill.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : He was a herbivore. He ate plants in a huge herd. [hesitant] T. rex hunted him: he was almost his favorite meal.
**WEX** [nervous] : T. rex ate him a lot?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Probably. [gently] There were so many of them, and a herd is like a walking buffet for a lone hunter.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : He had up to a THOUSAND small teeth. [pauses] When one fell out, a new one grew in right away.
**WEX** [gasps] : A thousand teeth?
**NARRATEUR H** [playful] : Yes, a real tooth [amazed] factory. He never ran out of teeth to grind up the toughest branches.
