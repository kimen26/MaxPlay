## Jobaria — Jobaria tiguidensis

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : jo… BAR… ee… ah. [curious] This name is a little different.
**WEX** [curious] : Is it Greek, like the others?
**NARRATEUR H** [playful] : Not this time. [warmly] It comes from Africa. The Tuareg, a people of the desert, told stories about the Jobar, a giant animal.
**WEX** [confident] : So when they found its huge bones, somebody thought of that giant.
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Exactly. [confident] It lived in Niger, a hundred and sixty-five million years ago, [pauses] in the Jurassic.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : 56 feet long — as long as a school bus and a half. [quickly] 15 feet tall — as tall as a double-decker bus. And about 35,000 pounds — [amazed] as heavy as 8 rhinos.
**WEX** [amazed] : Eight rhinos, all on four legs.
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Yes. [proud] Legs as strong as pillars.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : A plant-eater. It snipped off leaves and branches with its spoon-shaped teeth. [confident] Big and small Jobaria were found together, [curious] so it maybe lived in a herd.
**WEX** [curious] : Was its neck as long as Diplodocus's?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : A bit shorter. [softly] But long enough to reach way up above the ground. [nervous] And a hunter prowled nearby, Afrovenator. [serious] The grown-ups were probably too big for it, but the babies had to watch out.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Scientists found over ninety-five percent of its skeleton. [amazed] Almost ALL its bones, buried in the sand of the Sahara.
**WEX** [curious] : In the desert? But what did it eat out there?
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : Back then, it wasn't a desert. [excited] There were rivers, lakes and lots of plants. [warmly] The sand came much, much later.
