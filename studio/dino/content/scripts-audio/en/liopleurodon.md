## Liopleurodon — Liopleurodon ferox

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [curious] : Liopleurodon. Three little Greek pieces: "leios" means smooth. [serious] "Pleura," the side. "Odon," tooth.
**WEX** [confident] : Teeth... smooth on the side?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Right — no little ridges on them, like some shark teeth have. He lived one hundred sixty-five million years ago, [amazed] in the seas of Europe, [confident] in France and England.
**WEX** [curious] : And how did he hunt, with a lizard's nose?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Not with his eyes first — [serious] with his sense of smell. His nostrils pointed forward, like a dog tracking a scent.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : He was 7 meters long — like two cars, one behind the other! [amazed] He was as tall as a car — you'd have to look up! And he weighed 5,000 kilos — [serious] as heavy as an elephant!
**WEX** [gasps] : A swimming elephant?
**NARRATEUR H** [chuckles] : A swimming elephant, yes — with a real short neck [amazed] and a jaw the size of a bathtub.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : He hunted alone, and in his sea, he was hunter number one. [confident] Giant fish, other marine reptiles — [proud] almost nothing could stand up to him.
**WEX** [curious] : The boss of the whole sea?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : The boss, yes. Mostly thanks to his nose — [proud] he smelled his prey before he ever saw it.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [playful] : A TV show once said he was 82 feet long — like four cars end to end. [amazed] Real scientists checked: that's wrong. [serious] Seven meters, not more.
**WEX** [chuckles] : Even TV can get it wrong?
**NARRATEUR H** [warmly] : Even TV. Seven meters is already one seriously scary hunter — [confident] no need to make it bigger.
