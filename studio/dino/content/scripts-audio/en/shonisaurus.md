## Shonisaurus — Shonisaurus popularis

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [curious] : SHOH… nih… SOR… us. His name comes from a place: the Shoshone Mountains, [serious] in the Nevada desert — that's where his bones were found.
**WEX** [confident] : The Shoshone Mountains lizard?
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Exactly. He lived two hundred thirty million years ago — [amazed] way before most of the dinosaurs you know.
**WEX** [curious] : A desert, but he lived in water?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Back then, that desert was a sea. [amazed] The whole region was underwater.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : He was 14 meters long — as long as a school bus! [amazed] He was as tall as a front door! And he weighed 25,000 kilos — [serious] as heavy as 5 elephants!
**WEX** [gasps] : Five elephants, in the water?
**NARRATEUR H** [chuckles] : Five swimming elephants, yes — [proud] one of the biggest animals in his whole ocean.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : He ate fish and cephalopods, squid's cousins. [confident] And once he was grown, he had no hunters left at all.
**WEX** [curious] : Nobody could eat him?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Nobody. At his size, [serious] there just wasn't anything strong enough to take him on.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [whispers] : In Nevada, in today's desert, scientists found 37 Shonisaurus skeletons all in the same spot. [amazed] Maybe they stranded together, [serious] the way whales sometimes do today.
**WEX** [gasps] : Thirty-seven at once?
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : Thirty-seven, yes. Scientists are still trying to figure out [curious] why they were all there together.
