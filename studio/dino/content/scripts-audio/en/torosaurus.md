## Torosaurus — Torosaurus latus

### BLOC A — Présentation

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : TOR-oh-SOR-us. [curious] In Greek, "toro," here, means punched-through, holed. Not "bull," even though it sounds like it. And "saurus" is lizard.
**WEX** [playful] : The holey lizard.
**NARRATEUR H** [happily] : Almost: the lizard with the punched-through frill. [warmly] He lived in North America, sixty-six million years ago, alongside Triceratops.
**WEX** [softly] : That's Tritri's cousin.
**NARRATEUR H** [warmly] : Exactly. A big cousin, with a frill full of holes.

### BLOC B — Taille

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : He was 8 meters long — like two cars, one behind the other! 3 meters tall — [quickly] as tall as a basketball hoop! [amazed] And 8,000 kilos — as heavy as 4 rhinos!
**WEX** [gasps] : Four rhinos.
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : Yes. And his frill alone, [proud] was almost 3 meters wide.

### BLOC C — Comment il vivait

**NARRATEUR H** [serious] : He was a herbivore. He ate plants and leaves, [calm] in small groups, like his horned cousins.
**WEX** [curious] : What were the holes in his frill for?
**NARRATEUR H** [confident] : To lighten it. [gently] A giant frill, but full of windows — less heavy to carry around all day.

### BLOC D — Le truc fou

**NARRATEUR H** [excited] : Torosaurus had one of the biggest heads of any animal that ever walked the Earth. [whispers] Bigger than an elephant's.
**WEX** [gasps] : Bigger than an elephant's head.
**NARRATEUR H** [softly] : Yes. A head almost as big as you are, all of you.
